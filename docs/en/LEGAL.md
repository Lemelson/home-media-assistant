# Legal considerations

[Русский](../ru/LEGAL.md) · Research date: 16 September 2026

This is general information about the public software release, not an individual legal opinion. Neither a license nor this document guarantees that no one will make a claim. The US, EU member states, the UK and other European jurisdictions are not one legal system. Applicable rules depend on location, conduct, audience, commercial use and the rights in each work. For a public or commercial deployment, get advice on the actual design and countries involved before launch.

## Content rights and distribution

Use your own material or works whose license/permission covers the intended copying, distribution and playback. Public-domain status can differ by country and by version, soundtrack or restoration. Record the source, rightsholder, exact license and conditions. Creative Commons restrictions such as attribution, noncommercial use or share-alike may matter. A search result, purchased copy, subscription, personal-use label or the age of a film does not establish a universal right to redistribute it.

The US Copyright Office explains that unauthorized uploading or downloading of protected works can infringe copyright. BitTorrent can upload pieces during downloading, so permission to download alone may be insufficient. See its [copyright FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html).

Distributing software can also create liability when the facts show active inducement of infringement; the US Supreme Court addressed this in [MGM v. Grokster, 545 U.S. 913 (2005), opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/04-480). In the EU, the Court examined active operation of an indexing and sharing platform in [Stichting Brein, C-610/15](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/jcms/p1_370399/en/). These decisions are context-specific, not a ruling on this project.

Accordingly, the release includes no media files, functioning torrent examples, default indexers or instructions for obtaining unauthorized copies. That is a concrete scope choice, not a legal safe harbor. Operators must verify their own sources; the code does not determine copyright status.

## Access controls and service terms

Do not use this project to circumvent DRM, subscriptions, account controls or access restrictions. US anti-circumvention rules are a distinct issue from whether a copy is infringing, and exemptions have conditions; see the [Copyright Office's Section 1201 overview](https://www.copyright.gov/policy/1201/). No bypass tools or configuration are included.

Install Telegram, Transmission, Plex, Prowlarr and any external services under their own current terms. This project is not affiliated with or endorsed by them. An API key is not permission to reuse every returned image. Enabling artwork requires checking provider attribution/branding requirements and applicable image rights; a text credit alone is not a blanket license. No third-party artwork or logos are bundled.

## Personal information

Publishing this repository does not by itself mean its maintainer receives every installation's runtime data. The code supplies no central telemetry endpoint. Operators still process Telegram identifiers, chat requests, filenames and local records, and configured external providers may receive some of them. Consult [the data-flow inventory](PRIVACY.md).

GDPR excludes certain purely personal or household processing, but that exception must not be assumed for a public service. Territorial scope, lawful basis, transparency, data minimization, retention, security, processors and international transfers may need assessment where GDPR applies. See the [GDPR text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng) and the [European Commission's practical overview](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/governance-and-sustainability/digital-and-data-compliance/data-protection-gdpr/index_en.htm). This document is not a completed privacy notice or data-processing agreement for someone else's service.

## Jurisdiction notes: United States, Russia and Europe

In the United States, the Copyright Office warns that unauthorized uploading or downloading can infringe reproduction or distribution rights. Owning software, paying for a subscription or calling the use private does not establish permission for a particular transfer. [Copyright Office FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html).

Russia's Civil Code article 1273 provides a limited personal-copying rule with conditions and exceptions. Article 1270 addresses separate forms of exploitation, including making works available. Do not treat personal copying as blanket authorization for public peer-to-peer uploading. Sources: [Rospatent's Civil Code publication](https://rospatent.gov.ru/ru/documents/grazhdanskiy-kodeks-rossiyskoy-federacii-chast-chetvertaya) and [current article 1273 text](https://www.consultant.ru/document/cons_doc_LAW_64629/f63562ebf49f4d5fbe0c3daa9ea22a689d2d64ab/). Check the applicable current law for a concrete use.

For the EU, the Court of Justice held in ACI Adam (C-435/12) that the private-copying framework cannot disregard whether the source is lawful. National implementation still matters. Europe includes non-EU countries with their own rules, so this is not a pan-European clearance. [Official judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/PDF/?uri=ecli%3AECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2014%3A254).

The MIT software license does not grant media rights, eliminate mandatory law or prevent claims. A guide cannot legalize unauthorized transfers. GitHub can restrict content following its [DMCA process](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy). A jurisdiction-specific review by qualified counsel is the appropriate next step for a legal opinion about an actual deployment. The repository publishes no assurance of immunity.
